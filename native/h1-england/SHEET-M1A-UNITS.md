# H1 England: sourcing sheet M1a units (measure-to-mass conversions for English grain measures)

Written 2026-11-03. Sheet only: no record, no code. PLAN §8 stage B (d), CAT-5; answers Government's GOV-26 and History's
I4. NATIVE-PLAN rulings (a)-(i) apply: a value is here only from a work opened in this session; a figure a work cites
from another work, or a work seen only as a search-result line, is `researched`/low and says so (rule a); a combination
no opened source gives is an `open` row naming what was searched (rule c). Every Wikipedia page, the Archives of
Maryland pages, the Rogers volume, the Philosophical Transactions pages and the archive.org text were read through
WebFetch, which returns the answer to a prompt about the page, not the page verbatim; the PDFs listed as "extracted
locally" were read as text with a PDF reader. Quotes below are the words that came back, kept short. Nothing is
computed: no midpoint, no per-grain average, no conversion between pounds and kilograms that a source does not print. The
units "Tower pound", "troy pound" and "avoirdupois pound" are three different pounds; the sheet keeps the one the source
names and never merges them. Statute citations are regnal, as the sources print them (a calendar year only where the
source gives one).

Reading the tables. `conversion: exact` marks a definitional relation (a statute or assize fixing one unit as so many of
another); `conversion: empirical` marks a measured or derived weight or capacity. A capacity in cubic inches is
`empirical` when it was measured on a surviving standard. `label` is `researched` on every row; `confidence` follows
rule (a). "Place" is `region.england` where the statute or assize binds the realm; elsewhere the place the source names.
Row ids are proposals; the keeper's records unit settles names. Work keys are proposals (Sources searched, last table).

## Conversions

One row per sourced mass for a measure of grain. All rows are `conversion: empirical` and `label: researched`. A range stays a range.
Only wheat has a mass that comes from a weighing; every other grain is `open` (rows O1-O9) or appears only in the rows
after the window or with no period. Where a source prints the mass but not the grain, the grain cell says "not stated".
The statutory masses (64 troy pounds of wheat per bushel and the like) are definitions, not weighings; they are in
Measures (M1-M7, `exact`), not here.

Not written as rows, with the reason:

- Wikipedia's Quarter page gives "about 494 lb (224 kg)" for an 1825 quarter of wheat; its footnote says this is the page's own calculation (a density of 0.770 from engineeringtoolbox.com applied to 64 imperial gallons), not a historical weighing.
- John Hearfield's web page "The price of a loaf of bread in the 18th and 19th centuries" (2009) says a bushel of wheat is "equivalent to 60 lb" and so a quarter "480 lb", but attributes the 60 lb to "several Internet sites"; no source, place or date.
- A mailing-list post (list-cumbria, 23 February 2024, Bruce McDowall) reads: "For wheat, a bushell was 60 pound, (oats 40 pound)", in a thread on a Carlisle newspaper of 1823; the post is a modern recollection, and the newspaper was not opened.
- The same author's other lines in that thread, and US and Canadian commodity weights (wheat 60, rye 56, barley 48, oats 32 or 34) on Wikipedia's Bushel page, are not English figures of the period and are not recorded.
- The 1775 article's count of 96 plump wheat grains in 1773, each parcel of 32 weighing 22½ troy grains, is a mass for a number of grains, not for a measure; it bears on the pennyweight of M5 and is not a row.
